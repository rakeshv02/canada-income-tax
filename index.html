How income tax works in Canada (2026)

Canada taxes income twice: once federally and once provincially, and both use progressive brackets. In 2026 the federal rates run from 14% (the completed transition from the 2025 mid-year cut) up to 33% above $258,482. Every province and territory then adds its own tax on top — which is why the same $75,000 salary leaves you with a different amount in Calgary than in Halifax.

On top of income tax, employees pay CPP contributions (5.95% of earnings between $3,500 and $74,600, plus a second 4% CPP2 tier up to $85,000) and EI premiums (1.63% up to $68,900 of insurable earnings). Quebec runs its own versions — QPP and QPIP — with slightly different rates, and Quebec residents get a 16.5% abatement on their federal tax.

Which province takes the least?

For most salaries, Alberta wins: an 8% starting rate, the country's highest basic personal amount ($22,323) and no provincial sales tax. The territories (Nunavut especially) are also light on tax. Quebec sits at the other end with a 14% starting rate — though Quebecers get the federal abatement and heavily subsidised childcare in return. Ontario looks moderate on paper but adds a surtax on higher incomes plus a health premium of up to $900.

FAQs

How much tax do I pay on $75,000 in Canada?

Roughly $18,000–$22,500 in combined tax, CPP and EI depending on the province — pick your province above for the exact figure. Alberta and the territories leave the most in your pocket; Quebec the least (before its abatement and social benefits).

What are CPP and EI in 2026?

CPP: 5.95% of earnings between $3,500 and $74,600, plus 4% CPP2 on earnings up to $85,000. EI: 1.63% of earnings up to $68,900. Quebec replaces these with QPP and QPIP at its own rates.

What is the federal basic personal amount in 2026?

The 2026 federal BPA is $16,452 for most taxpayers, phasing down to $14,829 for incomes above $181,440.

Are RRSP contributions included?

No — this shows tax before RRSP deductions. Every dollar you contribute to an RRSP reduces taxable income, so your marginal rate shown here is also your RRSP refund rate.
